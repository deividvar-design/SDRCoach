Never argue with the incumbent. The prospect chose it, so criticising it means criticising them. Ask two things instead: what they use it for, and what it does not cover. The first shows interest and gets them talking. The second finds the gap, and almost every tool has one. No gap means you learned that in two minutes. A gap is a reason for a follow-up that has nothing to do with replacing anything.

## What they actually mean

Often it is simply true. Most companies you call have something in place, because the problem is not new. "We already have a solution" is a statement of fact plus a request to stop pitching. It is one of the most common lines in [the objections reps hear most](/blog/common-cold-call-objections).

The honest version comes with a name, a date or a detail. They tell you who the vendor is, when they signed, or what it replaced. The brush-off is vaguer. "We've got something for that" often means they are not sure what the something is, or whether anyone still uses it.

The signal under both versions is "I am not starting from zero, so do not treat me as if I were". Reps who do well treat the incumbent as a fact about the prospect's world, like headcount, and ask about it with the same curiosity. Reps who do badly hear a competitor's name and start comparing.

## Three ways to handle it

### Ask what they use it for

The default. It shows respect, gets specifics, and tells you where your product does and does not overlap.

> Makes sense, most teams have something. What do you mainly use it for?

### Ask what it does not cover

Use this once they have described it. Every tool has edges, and the daily user knows where they are.

> And what's the bit it doesn't really do? The thing someone still ends up doing in a spreadsheet.

### Park the contract and book the comparison

Use this when they are locked in until a date. Do not fight it. Aim to be the comparison they run before renewal, not a replacement they consider now. If the real worry is the effort of changing, that is [a switching objection](/objections/too-hard-to-switch) and needs its own answer.

> Understood, nothing changes before June. When do you start reviewing it, March?
>
> Let's talk a month before that, so you know what to compare it against.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** We already have a tool for that.
>
> **Rep:** Makes sense. What do you mainly use it for?
>
> **Prospect:** Tracking who's called who, mostly. Activity reporting for the board.
>
> **Rep:** And the bit it doesn't do? Where does someone still end up in a spreadsheet?
>
> **Prospect:** Coaching, honestly. It tells me how many calls, not whether any of them were any good.
>
> **Rep:** That's the piece I'm calling about, and it sits next to what you have rather than replacing it. Fifteen minutes Wednesday at eleven to show you?
>
> **Prospect:** Wednesday works.

A coach would score this well on discovery and on objection handling. No comparison, no criticism. Two questions let the prospect name the gap, and the value link was one sentence tied to what they said. "It sits next to what you have" took the switching fear off the table early.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "Which one? Oh, we're much better than them" | You have insulted the person who signed the contract. They defend the choice, and now you are arguing. |
| "How much are you paying for it?" | Too early and too personal. It sounds like you are about to undercut, which is a price pitch. |
| Listing features the incumbent lacks | You are guessing at gaps instead of asking, and the prospect knows their tool better than you. |
| "When's your contract up?" as the first question | It says you only want the renewal. Ask what the tool does first, and the date becomes a natural question. |
| Giving up at "we have one" | Having one and being covered are different things. One more question finds out which. |

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "Who are you with?" | "What do you mainly use it for?" |
| "We're better than them because..." | "What's the bit it doesn't cover?" |
| "We'd replace that" | "This sits next to what you have." |
| "When's the contract up?" | "When do you start reviewing it?" |
