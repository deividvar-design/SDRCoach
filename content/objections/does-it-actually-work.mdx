When a prospect doubts that what you sell works, give them one specific proof point for a company like theirs, then ask what would convince them. "Fair question. A team about your size in your sector saw this result in this many weeks. What would you need to see to believe it for your lot?" One real example, with the detail that makes it real, does more than a page of claims. The question afterwards matters just as much, because the prospect tells you what proof they want, and you spend the rest of the call on that instead of guessing.

## What they actually mean

Doubt is interest with its guard up. Nobody doubts a thing they do not care about. They say [not interested](/objections/not-interested) and go. "Does it actually work" means "I can picture using this, and I have been burnt before." That history is usually real: a tool bought on a promise that nobody used, a vendor who oversold, a project that quietly died. Our list of [common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections) puts this one among the better ones to hear.

The honest version comes with a story or a specific question. "Does it work with a team of three?" "What happens in month four when the novelty wears off?" "We tried one two years ago and nobody logged in." The brush-off version is flat and general, "everyone says that", and the prospect does not wait for your answer.

The tell is what happens after your proof point. The honest doubter listens to it and asks a follow-up. The brush-off talks over it. If you get the follow-up, you are in a sales conversation. If you get talked over, close politely and move on.

## Three ways to handle it

### One proof point, then ask what would convince them

The default. Use your own real example, and keep it to one. A second example sounds like a list, and lists sound rehearsed.

> Fair question. A team about your size in logistics went from six weeks to four on ramp time.
>
> What would you need to see to believe it for your team?

### Ask about the last time it did not work

Use this when they mention a past failure, or when the doubt sounds personal. It gets the real fear into the open and tells you what to avoid promising.

> Sounds like you have been burnt before. What did the last one promise, and where did it fall down?

### Agree and shrink the bet

Use this when the proof point landed but they still hesitate. Make the next step so small that doubt is more expensive than trying it.

> I would not take my word for it either.
>
> Run it with one rep this week. If nothing changes, you have lost twenty minutes.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** Everyone says it works. Does it, actually?
>
> **Rep:** Honest answer, not for everyone. Where it has worked is teams under ten where the manager actually listens to calls. Is that you?
>
> **Prospect:** That is us. We tried a coaching tool two years ago and nobody used it.
>
> **Rep:** What did it ask the reps to do?
>
> **Prospect:** Fill in a form after every call. Lasted a fortnight.
>
> **Rep:** Right, that is the usual reason. This one does not ask them to type anything. Run one rep through it this week and judge it on whether they do a second one. Thursday at ten to set it up?
>
> **Prospect:** Fine. Thursday.

A coach would score this well on objection handling and on tone. The rep conceded a limit, "not for everyone", which is what made the proof believable. They asked about the past failure instead of arguing with it, answered the real fear in one sentence, and sized the next step to the doubt. Nothing in the rep's voice went up when the prospect pushed, and that calm is most of why the prospect kept talking.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Yes, absolutely, it works really well." | A claim, answering a doubt about claims. No evidence was added. |
| Three logos and five statistics in a row | Sounds rehearsed. One example with detail beats a list every time. |
| "Our customers see huge results." | Cannot be checked, so the prospect does not try. "Huge" is not a number. |
| Getting defensive | Your tone goes up, and the prospect's guard goes up with it. |
| Giving the proof and moving straight on | You never learnt what proof they wanted, so the next thing you say is a guess. |

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Trust me, it works." | "A team like yours saw this. What would convince you for your team?" |
| "We have loads of case studies." | "One example, then you tell me if it is close enough to your setup." |
| "That will not happen with us." | "What did the last one get wrong? I will tell you honestly if we do the same." |
| "You will see results in the first month." | "Try it with one rep for a week and judge it on whether they do a second." |

The Level 3 cold prospect challenges every claim you make. Dial it and count how many claims you make before you offer a single specific example. Aim for one claim, one example, one question.
