Do not argue the fit. Say "that may well be right", then ask one question about how they handle the problem today. If the answer confirms they are outside what you sell to, thank them and end the call well. If it does not, you have a reason to continue. This works because the prospect knows their business and you have known them for thirty seconds. One question puts the facts on the table without a fight.

## What they actually mean

Three things hide behind these four words. Sometimes they are right. They have no team or no use case, and talking does not change it. Sometimes they have misunderstood you. One word in your opener sounded like a category they already ruled out. And sometimes it is a polite version of not interested, dressed up as a judgement about fit because that is harder to argue with.

The question sorts them. The honest version answers with something specific: "we only hire two reps a year and the manager sits with them". The brush-off answers with something vague: "it's just not for us". Vague answers mean the fit was never the issue, and you should treat it as you would [not interested](/objections/not-interested).

A rep who ends a genuine no-fit call in forty seconds has done better than one who kept the prospect talking for ten minutes.

## Three ways to handle it

### Ask how they handle it today

Use this first, every time.

> That may well be right. Can I ask one thing before I go? How do you handle ramp for new reps today?

### Check what they heard

Use this when the objection came straight after your opener and you suspect they ruled out a category, not you.

> So I do not waste your time, when you say not a fit, is that about size, or how the team is set up?

### Thank them and name the trigger

Use this when the answer confirms no fit. Leave one specific reason to call again.

> That is a clear answer and I appreciate it.
>
> If you get to five or six reps a year, I would like to be the first call. Thanks for your time.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** We're not a fit for this, honestly.
>
> **Rep:** That may well be right. Can I ask one thing before I go? How do you handle ramp for new reps today?
>
> **Prospect:** We don't, really. We hire two a year and the manager sits with them.
>
> **Rep:** Two a year. That is below where this makes sense, you are right. Is that changing in the next twelve months?
>
> **Prospect:** We are hiring six in January, as it happens.
>
> **Rep:** Then January is when it stops being a one-manager job. Can I call you in the first week of December, before they start?
>
> **Prospect:** That is reasonable.
>
> **Rep:** First week of December. I am putting it in now. Thanks for the straight answer.

A coach scores this well on discovery and closing. The rep accepted the prospect's reality, asked one question that got a real answer, spotted the change in timing, and left with a date.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "Actually, we work with companies just like yours" | This is arguing the fit. The prospect said no and the rep said they were wrong. |
| "What makes you say that?" in a flat tone | The words are fine. The tone turns a question into a cross-examination. |
| "Let me just explain what we do" | They have already decided. A longer pitch confirms the decision rather than reversing it. |
| "No problem, have a good day" straight away | Skips the one question that would have found the January hires. |
| "I'll send some information over anyway" | Nobody reads it. The prospect learns that no costs nothing. |

Reps either fight this objection or fold on it. One question, then a decision, is the move in the middle. It is in our [list of common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections) for a reason.

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "We actually work with a lot of companies your size" | "That may well be right. How do you handle that today?" |
| "You might be surprised" | "When you say not a fit, is that about size, or how the team is set up?" |
| "Are you sure?" | "That is a clear answer. Thanks for it." |
| "Can I send something over just in case?" | "If that changes, I would like to be the first call. Can I check in in December?" |
