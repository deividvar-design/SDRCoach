"Send me an email" is a polite way to end the call, and the right response is to agree and then ask what would make the email worth reading. Say "Happy to. So it is not one you delete, what is the one thing you would want it to answer?" That question makes the prospect describe their situation out loud, which is the discovery you were trying to start anyway. If they give you something real, you have a conversation. If they say "anything is fine", you have learnt they were never a prospect, and you can stop spending dials on them.

## What they actually mean

Most of the time the line means "I would like this call to end without being rude." The prospect has no picture of what you do and no reason yet to form one. An email is the cheapest way to get you off the phone, and they expect you to take it. It is the most common soft exit on our list of [common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections), and the one reps fold on fastest.

Sometimes it is honest. A manager who is mildly interested but in the middle of something will ask for an email so they can read it when they have a minute. You can tell the difference by what comes with the request. The honest version has a detail attached: "send me the pricing" or "send me how it works with our dialler". The brush-off has nothing attached: "just send whatever you have."

Your question is the test. Ask what the email should answer. A real prospect answers in a few seconds because they already have a question in their head. A brush-off stalls, repeats the request, or says "anything". Treat that as the same signal as [not interested](/objections/not-interested) and end the call well.

## Three ways to handle it

### Agree, then ask what would make it worth reading

The default. Use it on every first "send me an email" unless the prospect has already named a topic.

> Happy to. So it is not one you delete, what is the one thing you would want it to answer?

### Answer the question now

Use this when they name a topic. Whatever they asked for, you can usually say in one sentence, and saying it keeps the conversation alive.

> I can send that. The short version is it runs per seat, usually forty to eighty a month.
>
> Want me to tell you where you would land? Takes two questions.

### Trade the email for a time

Use this when they have engaged a little but keep drifting back to the email. It turns a vague follow-up into a yes-or-no question.

> Rather than go back and forth over email, I have fifteen minutes on Thursday at ten.
>
> If it is not relevant you will know in five.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** Look, just send me an email and I will have a look.
>
> **Rep:** Happy to. So it is not one you delete, what is the one thing you would want it to answer?
>
> **Prospect:** Whether it actually works with the dialler we already use.
>
> **Rep:** Fair question. It sits alongside the dialler rather than replacing it. Which one are you on?
>
> **Prospect:** A cloud one, nothing fancy. Been on it two years.
>
> **Rep:** Then it will. Quickest way to check is fifteen minutes on Thursday at ten. Does that work, or is Friday better?
>
> **Prospect:** Thursday is fine. Send the invite.

A coach would score this well on objection handling. The rep acknowledged the request, isolated the real concern with one question, and redirected into discovery without defending the product. The close is clean too: a specific slot, offered as a choice, confirmed by the prospect in their own words. There is a longer walk-through of the same four steps in our post on [handling send me an email without sending the email](/blog/handling-send-me-an-email).

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Sure, what is the best address?" | You have accepted the no. The email goes unread and the call gets logged as progress when it was a loss. |
| "I would rather not send an email, can I just explain quickly?" | Refusing a reasonable request makes you sound scripted, and the prospect digs in. |
| "What is it you want to know?" | Too open. The prospect says "everything" or "whatever you have", and you are back where you started. |
| A long email with three attachments | Nobody reads it. If you must send one, send three sentences that answer what they asked, with a time at the bottom. |
| "I will send it over and call you next week." | Two vague commitments stacked on each other. Neither has a time, so neither happens. |

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Sure, what is your email?" | "Happy to. What is the one thing you would want it to answer?" |
| "I would rather not, it is easier to explain on the phone." | "I can. Quick question first so I send the right thing." |
| "I will send some information over." | "I will send three lines on the thing you asked. Then can we take fifteen minutes Thursday?" |
| "Let me know if you have any questions." | "I will call you Tuesday at eleven to see if it landed." |

<Practise>
Ask the Level 2 inbound prospect a discovery question and it will ask you to send something within a minute. Run it five times, agree every time, and count how many of those five you turn into a question the prospect has to answer.
</Practise>
