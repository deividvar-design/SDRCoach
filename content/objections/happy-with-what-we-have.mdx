When a prospect says they are happy with what they have, agree, then ask what they would change if they could. "Good to hear. If you could change one thing about it, what would it be?" Nobody is completely content with a tool, a process or a supplier, and the exception they name is the only part of their world you should talk about for the rest of the call. Arguing that they should be less happy is a losing position. Asking about the exception is not an argument, it is curiosity, and most people answer it.

## What they actually mean

"Happy" is rarely a review. It is the shortest way to say "I have no reason to think about this right now." The prospect has something that works well enough, no pain loud enough to act on, and no energy for a comparison with a stranger on the phone. It sits near the top of our list of [common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections) because it is true of most people most of the time.

The honest version has a choice behind it. They moved to something last year, it was a decision, and they will tell you about it if you ask the right way. The brush-off version is generic: no named tool, no detail, just "we're all set". The honest version will name an exception within a few seconds of being asked. The brush-off repeats that everything is fine and waits for you to go.

This is a different objection from [we already have a solution](/objections/we-already-have-a-solution), even though reps often treat them the same. The solution objection is about the tool and invites a comparison. Happy is about the mood. Comparisons do not move a mood, and a feature list makes it worse. The exception question is the only thing we have seen work reliably.

## Three ways to handle it

### Ask what they would change

The default. Light, curious, and almost impossible to refuse.

> Good to hear, most people are not. If you could change one thing about it, what would it be?

### Ask what happy is measured against

Use this when the prospect sounds senior and precise. It respects the claim and asks for the evidence behind it, which they will usually have.

> Fair enough. What does happy look like for you? Time saved, cost, or just no complaints from the team?

### Name the exception for them

Use this when you know the category well and have heard the same gap from several peers. It is our observation that a specific, plausible gap gets a more honest answer than an open question, but only if you are right. If you are guessing, use the first move.

> Makes sense. The one thing teams on that kind of setup usually mention is the reporting.
>
> Is that true for you, or have you cracked it?

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** We're happy with what we have, honestly.
>
> **Rep:** Good to hear. If you could change one thing about it, what would it be?
>
> **Prospect:** Hmm. Onboarding for new starters is slow, I suppose. Takes a couple of weeks before they are useful on it.
>
> **Rep:** How many new starters a quarter?
>
> **Prospect:** Four or five.
>
> **Rep:** So roughly ten weeks a quarter of people half-working. That is the only bit I would want to show you. Fifteen minutes next Tuesday at two?
>
> **Prospect:** Fine, send it over.

A coach would score this well on discovery. The rep never disagreed with "happy". One question got the exception, one follow-up turned it into a number the prospect produced themselves, and the rep then narrowed the next step to that one thing rather than opening it back up to a general demo. The tone stayed level throughout, which is what made the questions sound like interest rather than a trap.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Have you compared it to anything recently?" | Implies they are complacent. The prospect now defends the choice harder than they would have. |
| "A lot of our customers said that before they switched." | Smug, and it tells the prospect you have already decided they are wrong. |
| Listing features the current tool lacks | You are guessing. Every wrong guess is proof you do not know their setup. |
| "No problem, I will leave you to it." | Folds on a line that was never a hard no. Nothing was learnt and nothing was booked. |
| "What if I could show you something better?" | A hypothetical with no content. The easiest thing in the world to say no to. |

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Are you sure you have looked at the alternatives?" | "If you could change one thing about it, what would it be?" |
| "Most people who say that end up switching." | "Good to hear. What does happy look like for you?" |
| "We are better than what you have got." | "The one thing teams on that setup mention is reporting. True for you?" |
| "OK, no problem, bye." | "Fair enough. One last thing, what would need to go wrong for you to look again?" |

<Practise>
The Level 3 cold prospect tells you it already has something and is fine with it, usually inside the first minute. Dial it until you can get the exception out of it three calls running without disagreeing once.
</Practise>
