Tell them. One sentence, the real source, no hedging: "Your profile is on LinkedIn and the number came from a list provider we license." Then go back to why you called, because that is what they are really asking. Most prospects are checking whether you are a scammer or a real person with a reason. Answer plainly, apologise at most once, and get to the reason.

## What they actually mean

Three versions of this question sound the same. The first is a security check. People get fraud calls every week and want to know which kind this is. The second is irritation. You rang a personal number mid-task and they want you to know it. The third is a test. Senior buyers ask it to see whether you stumble.

The honest version waits for your answer and then listens to the reason. The brush-off does not wait. It is followed in the same breath by "take me off your list", and the number was never the point.

If you sell in the EU, the honest answer usually names the source: a public profile, a company website, or a list provider. Our understanding is that GDPR allows B2B outreach on legitimate interest as long as the person can object, so the plain answer is also the safe one.

## Three ways to handle it

### Name the source, then the reason

Use this by default. The answer takes four seconds.

> Your profile on LinkedIn, and the number came from a data provider we license.
>
> The reason I rang is your team has grown this year, and ramp is usually where that hurts.

### Own the interruption once, then ask

Use this when it is their personal mobile and the irritation is real.

> Fair, I did interrupt you, and it is a cold call. Thirty seconds on why, then you decide.
>
> How are you getting new reps up to speed at the moment?

### Offer the exit, then earn thirty seconds

Use this when they ask to be removed from your list. The exit has to be real.

> I can take you off right now, and I will.
>
> Before I do, thirty seconds on why I called. If it is not relevant, you will not hear from me again.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** Hang on, how did you get this number?
>
> **Rep:** Your profile on LinkedIn, and the number came from a data provider we use. It is a cold call, I will be straight about that.
>
> **Prospect:** Right. I don't usually take these.
>
> **Rep:** Understood. The reason I called is you have hired about a dozen reps since spring, and ramp is usually where that hurts. Is that close?
>
> **Prospect:** Closer than I would like.
>
> **Rep:** Then it is worth fifteen minutes. Thursday at ten or Friday at two, whichever is less painful.
>
> **Prospect:** Friday.
>
> **Rep:** Friday at two. I will send the invite now.

A coach scores this well on objection handling and reason for call. The rep answered without defending, acknowledged the interruption once, tied the reason to the prospect's team, and closed with two times.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "I'm so sorry, I really didn't mean to bother you" | A second apology tells the prospect the call was a mistake. They agree and hang up. |
| "I'm not sure, it was just in our system" | Sounds evasive. The prospect now believes you are hiding something. |
| "It's publicly available information" | True, and combative. It argues with them instead of answering them. |
| "Anyway, can I ask you a couple of questions?" | They asked a direct question and you skipped it. |
| "We got it from a partner" when it came from a list | A lie that is easy to catch. One wrong answer and nothing else you say is trusted. |

The same mistake sinks [why are you calling me](/objections/why-are-you-calling-me). Both are in our [list of common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections).

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "It's on the internet" | "Your LinkedIn profile, and the number came from a list provider we license." |
| "Sorry, sorry, I know this is a cold call" | "Fair. It is a cold call, and I will be quick." |
| "I can't disclose that" | "A B2B data provider we license. I can send you the name if you want it." |
| "Anyway, as I was saying" | "That is how I have it. The reason I rang is this." |
