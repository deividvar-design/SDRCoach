Say in one sentence why their role made you call, then ask who owns the outcome. "I called you because you run the SDR team, and ramp time usually lands on whoever does. Is that you, or does someone else own it?" If the answer is someone else, get the name and a reason to call that person. That counts as a win. This works because the question is about relevance, not identity. A rep who cannot explain in one breath why this person deserves the call has just admitted they are dialling a list.

## What they actually mean

The question is almost always genuine. The prospect has a job title and a list of what crosses their desk. You rang about something that did not appear to be on it, and they want to know whether you did your homework or got lucky with a directory. Senior buyers also use it to see how you react under pressure.

The honest version waits for your answer and then either confirms they own it or tells you who does. The brush-off is the same words with no pause, often followed by "I'm not the person" before you have said anything. Even then, the one-sentence answer is worth giving, because it earns the referral.

Everything here depends on a [reason for the call](/blog/reason-for-call) that connects their role to an outcome. If your opener was "I'm calling to introduce myself", this objection has no answer.

## Three ways to handle it

### Tie the role to the outcome in one sentence

Use this every time, before anything else.

> Because you run the SDR team, and ramp time usually lands on whoever does. Is that you, or does someone else own it?

### Ask who owns the outcome

Use this when they say it is not them. Ask who owns the thing you called about, not who the decision maker is.

> Who does own ramp for new reps here? And what would they need to hear for the call to be worth their time?

### Take the name and the reason

Use this once you have the name. Permission to say who pointed you there makes the next call a referral.

> Thanks. When I call Sam, can I say it was you who pointed me there, and that ramp is the thing to raise?

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** Why are you calling me? I'm in ops, not sales.
>
> **Rep:** Because ops often ends up owning the sales tooling. If that is not you here, I would rather find out who it is than waste your time.
>
> **Prospect:** It is not me. That is Sam, who runs enablement.
>
> **Rep:** Sam. Does Sam own ramp for new reps, or just the content side?
>
> **Prospect:** Ramp, definitely. It is a sore point at the moment.
>
> **Rep:** That is useful. When I call Sam, can I say you pointed me there, and that ramp is the sore point?
>
> **Prospect:** Go ahead. Extension 214.
>
> **Rep:** Thanks. I will call this afternoon.

A coach scores this well on reason for call and objection handling. The rep gave a one-sentence reason, did not pitch the wrong person, asked who owns the outcome, and left with a name, a reason, and permission to use the referral.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "I'm calling everyone in your department" | Admits there was no reason to call this person. The prospect stops listening. |
| "Your name came up on our list" | A list is not a reason. It confirms the suspicion that you did no homework. |
| Pitching anyway | Ten minutes spent on someone who said they do not own the problem. Nothing moves. |
| "Can you transfer me to the right person?" | Without a name, this gets you a voicemail. Ask who owns it first. |
| "Who's your boss?" | The boss rarely owns the outcome either. |

Who owns the outcome is the same question you ask when a prospect is [not the decision maker](/objections/not-the-decision-maker). Both are in our [list of common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections).

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "Your name came up" | "You run the SDR team, and ramp lands on whoever does." |
| "Are you the decision maker?" | "Who owns ramp for new reps here?" |
| "Could you forward this on?" | "When I call Sam, can I say you pointed me there?" |
| "Sorry to bother you then" | "Thanks, that is exactly what I needed." |

<Practise>
Dial the Level 3 cold prospect, which asks who you are and why you are calling in the first ten seconds. Aim to say the role-to-outcome sentence before it asks twice, and to leave with a confirmed owner or a name.
</Practise>
