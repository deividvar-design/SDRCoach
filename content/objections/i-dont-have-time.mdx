Ask for thirty seconds, not fifteen minutes. When a prospect says they do not have time, the instinct is to ask for more of it, which is the wrong direction. Say "that's fair, give me thirty seconds and you can decide", then spend those seconds on a reason for the call that fits their role. A specific reason earns the extension. A generic pitch proves they were right to end the call.

## What they actually mean

Nobody has time for a call they did not expect. "I don't have time" is a reflex that fires before the prospect has worked out who you are. It is not a judgement on your product, because they have not heard about it yet. It is a judgement on the first ten seconds, and most cold calls sound alike for the first ten seconds. It is near the top of [the objections reps hear most](/blog/common-cold-call-objections), and in our observation the one reps fold on fastest.

The honest version comes with a detail. "I'm walking into a board meeting" or "I've got a customer on the other line" is real, and you should treat it as real. The brush-off is vague. "Now's not good", with no reason and a flat tone, is the prospect waiting to see whether you have a point.

The tell is what happens after you give the point. The honest prospect still has to go, but often says "call me at four" unprompted. The brush-off softens, because you just became the one caller today who was specific about their world.

## Three ways to handle it

### Trade down to thirty seconds

Use this as the default. It costs the prospect almost nothing and puts the decision back in their hands.

> That's fair. Give me thirty seconds to say why I called, then you can tell me to go.

### Spend the seconds on their world

Use this when they sound busy but not annoyed. Spend the seconds on a reason that fits their role, not on your company.

> I'm calling because you're hiring reps this quarter, and the first ninety days is usually where that goes wrong.
>
> If that's not on your plate, I'll leave you to it.

### Take the real no and pin a time

Use this when they give you a concrete reason. Do not fight it. You are now handling [a request to call back later](/objections/call-me-back-later), and the only good outcome is a time.

> Understood, go. Does four o'clock today work, or is tomorrow morning better? I'll keep it to five minutes.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** I don't have time for this.
>
> **Rep:** That's fair. Thirty seconds and you can tell me to go.
>
> **Prospect:** Go on then.
>
> **Rep:** You're hiring three reps this quarter. Most teams lose the first two months to ramp. I'm calling to ask how you're handling that.
>
> **Prospect:** Honestly, badly. They listen to calls and copy whoever's been here longest.
>
> **Rep:** That's what most teams do. Fifteen minutes Thursday at ten and I'll show you what the ones who ramp faster do differently. Does that work?
>
> **Prospect:** Thursday's fine. Send an invite.

A coach would score this well on reason for call and on objection handling. The rep acknowledged the objection, asked for something small, and spent it on the prospect's quarter rather than a pitch. The question did the work, and the close named a slot.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "It'll only take fifteen minutes" | Fifteen minutes is the thing they said they did not have. You have repeated the problem back at them. |
| "I'll be really quick", followed by ninety seconds of pitch | They are timing you. Pass the limit you set and you sound like every other caller. |
| "When would be a better time?" | Vague. They say "next week" and you both know it is a no with better manners. |
| "I understand you're busy, but..." | Everything before "but" is deleted. You acknowledged and ignored them in one breath. |
| Hanging up straight away | Polite, and it loses the call. In our observation most "no time" is a test, not a fact. |

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "Do you have a few minutes?" | "Thirty seconds, then you decide." |
| "I'll be quick. We help companies like yours..." | "I'm calling because you're hiring reps and ramp is where that goes wrong." |
| "When's a better time?" | "Four today or nine tomorrow? Five minutes." |
