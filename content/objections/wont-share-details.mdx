Stop asking for the number. Say "that is fair", offer a range you have seen from similar teams, and ask whether they sit above or below it. "Most teams we speak to take three to five months to get a rep to quota. Are you above or below that?" This works because nobody gives figures to a stranger, and pushing makes you sound like you need the number more than you need them. A range lets them stay vague while still telling you what you need.

## What they actually mean

This is a trust signal, not a refusal to talk. Three things sit behind it. The first is that they do not know you yet, and the question was too big for minute one. "What is your ramp time" is a second-meeting question. The second is policy. Some organisations tell staff not to share internals with vendors. The third is that they are not interested and the refusal is a convenient wall.

The honest version will react to a range. Offer "three to five months" and they will say "above" or "about that", because that costs them nothing. The brush-off refuses the range too and will not engage with the problem in any form. At that point you are handling [not interested](/objections/not-interested), and the move is different.

In our observation, the reps who get the most from a first call ask the fewest direct questions about figures. They ask about direction and about what annoys the prospect. The numbers come later.

## Three ways to handle it

### Offer a range and ask which side

Use this first. The range has to be real or it will not survive the next question.

> Completely fair. The teams we talk to usually take three to five months to get a rep to quota.
>
> Are you above or below that?

### Ask for the direction, not the figure

Use this when even the range gets a shrug.

> I do not need the number. Is it getting better or worse this year?

### Drop it and go back to the problem

Use this when they will not give a range or a direction. Mean it.

> Understood, I will leave numbers out of it. What is the part of ramp that annoys you most?

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** I'm not sharing numbers with someone I have never spoken to.
>
> **Rep:** Completely fair, and I will not ask again. The teams we talk to usually take three to five months to get a rep to quota. Are you above or below that?
>
> **Prospect:** Above. Comfortably.
>
> **Rep:** Above. Is that getting better or worse?
>
> **Prospect:** Worse. We have grown and the managers cannot listen to every call.
>
> **Rep:** That is the thing I called about. I would like to show you how one team got coaching time back without listening to everything. Twenty minutes on Wednesday morning?
>
> **Prospect:** Wednesday at ten, and I am still not giving you numbers.
>
> **Rep:** Wednesday at ten, no numbers. Invite on its way.

A coach scores this well on objection handling and discovery. The rep dropped the direct ask and did not return to it, used a range the prospect could answer without disclosing anything, got a direction, heard the real problem, and moved to a specific slot.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "Just a ballpark?" | Still asking for the number. The prospect has already said no to this. |
| "It would really help me help you" | Makes the call about what the rep needs. |
| "Other companies share this all the time" | Shames them for a reasonable decision. Nobody responds to shame by opening up. |
| "Why not?" | Challenges the decision. Now they have to defend it, which makes it permanent. |
| Going quiet and launching into the pitch | Tells the prospect the questions were a formality. |

Each of these keeps the pressure on the figure. The same instinct that takes it off helps with doubts about whether it works. Both are in our [list of common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections).

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "Roughly how many reps do you have?" | "Most teams we speak to are between ten and thirty reps. Which side are you on?" |
| "Can you give me a rough figure?" | "I do not need a figure. Better or worse than last year?" |
| "I need that to know if we can help" | "Understood, I will leave numbers alone. What annoys you most about it?" |
| "Come on, it's only a ballpark" | "That is fair. Above or below five months?" |
