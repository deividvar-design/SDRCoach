"Call me back later" is only worth anything if "later" has a date on it. Agree straight away, then ask for a specific day and time before you hang up: "No problem. Is Thursday at ten any good, or is the week after better?" A prospect who means it will pick one, because picking costs them nothing. A prospect who is brushing you off will stay vague, and that tells you the real answer is no, which saves you four more dials to a number that was never going to pick up.

## What they actually mean

There are three versions. The honest one is a prospect who is genuinely mid-task, walking into a meeting, or in a bad month. They will usually tell you why or when without being asked: "I am in a meeting", "after month end", "once the new system is in". The second is the deferral. They are not saying no, but you have given them no reason to say yes, so pushing you into the future is the easy option. The third is the brush-off, where later means never.

You tell them apart by how specific the later is. Honest comes with a time attached. Brush-off comes with "sometime", "a few weeks", "next quarter". Your job is the same in every case: put a time on it, and watch what the prospect does when you try. The honest ones help you. The brush-offs resist, and resistance is your answer.

One distinction matters. If the problem is the next two minutes rather than the next two months, you are dealing with a different objection. See [I don't have time](/objections/i-dont-have-time) for that one. Our list of [common cold-call objections](/blog/common-cold-call-objections) treats them separately for a reason.

## Three ways to handle it

### Agree and offer two slots

The default. An open "when suits you?" makes the prospect do the work, and they will not. Two options is a choice, not a chore.

> No problem. Is Thursday morning better, or early next week?
>
> I will put it in the diary now so neither of us has to chase.

### Ask what changes by then

Use this when they give a reason, like "after the quarter" or "when the new system is in". It tests whether the later is real, and it hands you the reason for the next call.

> Makes sense. What is different once the quarter closes that makes it a better conversation?

### Take thirty seconds now and book the rest

Use this when they are busy right now rather than busy this month. Earn a short slot, ask one sharp question, and book the proper call before you go.

> I will be thirty seconds. One question, and if it is relevant we book a proper time. If not, I am gone.

## An exchange that works

> **Prospect:** Can you call me back? This is not a great time.
>
> **Rep:** Of course. Is this a bad ten minutes or a bad month?
>
> **Prospect:** Bad month, honestly. We have an audit running until the end of October.
>
> **Rep:** Understood. Once the audit is done, is the first week of November any good? Wednesday the fourth at ten, say.
>
> **Prospect:** Wednesday is fine, but make it eleven.
>
> **Rep:** Eleven on the fourth. I will send a calendar hold now so it is in your diary. Thanks, speak then.

A coach would score this well on closing. The question "bad ten minutes or a bad month" isolated the objection in seven words and told the rep which version they were dealing with. The slot was specific, the prospect adjusted it, which means they own it, and the rep repeated it back and sent the hold before the call ended.

## The mistakes that lose the call

| What reps say | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Sure, I will try you again in a few weeks." | No date, no commitment. The next call is cold again, and you have taught the prospect that vague works. |
| "When would be a good time for you?" | Open question with no default. The prospect says "I don't know, just try me", and you are back to guessing. |
| "Is there a reason now is not good?" | Sounds like an accusation. Ask what changes by then, not why not now. |
| Pitching anyway, quickly | They said not now, and you proved you do not listen. The next call will not be answered. |
| A verbal time with no invite sent | A spoken slot decays by the hour. If it is not in their calendar it does not exist. |

## Say this, not that

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "I will try you again later in the month." | "Is the twenty-third at two any good? I will send a hold." |
| "When would suit you?" | "Thursday at ten, or Monday at three?" |
| "Why isn't now a good time?" | "What changes by then that makes it a better conversation?" |
| "No worries, sorry to bother you." | "No problem. Let me get a time in the diary and I will leave you to it." |
