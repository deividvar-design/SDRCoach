The eight cold call objections reps hear most are "I don't have time", "I'm not interested", "just send me an email", "we already have a solution", "there's no budget", "I'm not the right person", "call me back later" and "how did you get my number". Most are reflexes rather than decisions, offered because the prospect was interrupted, and the first objection on a call is rarely the real one. The response to each has the same shape: acknowledge it in a line, ask one question to find out whether it is the real objection, reframe it, then redirect to the reason for the call or a small next step. [Gong's analysis of 300 million cold calls](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all) found the five most common objections account for 74% of all objections and about half are brush-offs, so a rep who can handle a short list can handle most calls. Below is the one-line response to each, then the longer version.

## The eight at a glance

| Objection | What it usually means | The one-line response |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't have time" | They want to know how long this will take | "Fair, you weren't expecting me. Thirty seconds on why I called, then hang up on me if it's not useful?" |
| "I'm not interested" | You have not said anything worth being interested in yet | "That's fair, I haven't told you anything yet. The reason I called is..." |
| "Just send me an email" | A polite no | "Happy to. What's the one thing it would need to answer so you don't delete it?" |
| "We already have a solution" | Something is in place and changing it is work | "Good, so you've already decided this matters. What would you change about it if you could?" |
| "There's no budget" | Nobody has made the case yet, or they do not hold the budget | "Understood. No budget this year, or no budget until someone shows it pays for itself?" |
| "I'm not the right person" | Sometimes true, sometimes a polite exit | "Thanks for saying so. Who owns this at the moment, is that Ops or Finance?" |
| "Call me back later" | A delay they hope you forget | "Will do. Tuesday at ten, or Thursday at two?" |
| "How did you get my number?" | They are annoyed and testing whether you will be straight | "It's on your company's site. The reason I'm calling is..." |

Each objection also has its own page in the [objection library](/objections).

## The move, once

Every response uses the same four-part shape. Acknowledge: one short line that shows you heard them and will not argue. Isolate: a question that finds out whether this objection is the real one. Reframe: one sentence that puts it in a different light. Redirect: back to the reason for the call, or to a small next step.

Skip the first and you sound like a telemarketer. Skip the second and you spend the call answering an objection they did not mean. [Gong found](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-objections) top reps answer an objection with a clarifying question 54.3% of the time, against 31% for average reps. Ask, then answer.

## "I don't have time right now"

It is rarely about the calendar. You interrupted them and they want to know how long this will take. [Cognism's 2024 study](https://www.cognism.com/state-of-cold-calling) of about 10,000 calls found "I'm busy" was the most common objection and ended 30% of calls.

Ask for thirty seconds and use thirty, not ninety. If they say no even to that, offer two times. A specific slot is a yes-or-no question. The [I don't have time](/objections/i-dont-have-time) page covers the version where they really are walking into a meeting.

## "I'm not interested"

On a cold call this usually arrives before the reason for calling, so it is a reaction to being sold to, not to what you sell.

Acknowledge, then get specific. "That's fair, I haven't told you anything yet. The reason I called is that you added two depots this year, and most ops leads at that stage are fighting with driver scheduling. Is that on your list, or is that not you?" Being corrected is a conversation.

If it comes after the reason for calling, isolate it: "Not interested in fixing it, or not interested in talking to a vendor about it?" Push back once, as the [not interested](/objections/not-interested) page says, and no more.

## "Just send me an email"

A polite no. The prospect expects you to take the address and go away.

Agree, then ask what to put in it. Whatever they say, answer it in one sentence on the call, then offer a fifteen-minute slot. We have a [full post on this one](/blog/handling-send-me-an-email), because it is the objection reps fold on most, and the [send me an email](/objections/send-me-an-email) page has the three-sentence email to send afterwards.

## "We already have a solution for that"

The strongest of the eight, because it is often true. Do not attack the incumbent. Ask about it. "How long have you had it?" and then "What's the one thing you'd change about it if you could?" Most people have an answer, and that answer is your reason for a fifteen-minute next step. If nothing needs changing, believe them and ask when the renewal is. The [we already have a solution](/objections/we-already-have-a-solution) page covers the under-contract version.

## "There's no budget for this"

Either nobody has made the case yet, or they are not the person who holds the budget. The question in the table finds out which. If it is the first, ask when planning happens and aim for that. If it is the second, that is what the meeting is for.

Never respond with a price. Budget is a priority question, not a price question. The [no budget](/objections/no-budget) page covers spending freezes.

## "I'm not the right person"

Sometimes exactly that. Sometimes it means "I am the right person and I would like you to go away". In Cognism's data "wrong person" ended 27% of calls, second only to "I'm busy".

Take it at face value and ask for the referral with a specific role: "Who owns fleet scheduling, is that Ops or does it sit with Finance?" Naming a role is easier to answer than "who should I talk to?". Before you go, ask what their own involvement is, because wrong people often feel the problem they do not sign for. The [not the decision maker](/objections/not-the-decision-maker) page has the warm handover.

## "Call me back later"

A delay they hope you will forget. Occasionally it is genuine, in which case they will give you a time.

Make it concrete: this week or next, then Tuesday at ten or Thursday at two. If they refuse to give a time, it was a no. Treat it as "not interested". When you do call back, open with it: "You asked me to call back Thursday, so this is that call." The [call me back later](/objections/call-me-back-later) page covers how many times to try.

## "How did you get my number?"

They are annoyed, and they are testing whether you will be straight with them.

Be straight. "It's on your company's site", or "a data provider we pay for, which I know isn't a great answer." Then "the reason I'm calling is..." and go. An honest answer, delivered without apology, usually resets the call. Apologise once at most, as the [how did you get my number](/objections/how-did-you-get-my-number) page puts it, and never twice.

## Practise the reflex, not the script

On a real call the objection arrives while you are still mid-sentence, from someone who sounds irritated, and the polished answer is replaced by "okay, no problem, sorry to bother you". The gap between knowing the words and saying them under pressure is the whole skill, and it only closes with repetition. The median successful cold call in Gong's data lasts 4.8 minutes and handles at least two objections, so the booked meeting is usually the third yes, not the first.
