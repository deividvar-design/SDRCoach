Nooks sells AI roleplay as a module inside its outbound dialler, on a custom quote, with a two-seat minimum. Orum sells it as an add-on to its dialler, per user, billed annually. Both are good choices if you are buying the dialler. If what you want is the practice and not the dialler, the alternative is a standalone cold call simulator: priced per seat, grounded in your own target accounts and transcripts, and bought by a sales manager in an afternoon. 100 Dials is one, at $79 per seat per month with a free trial. Exec and Hyperbound are others, built for broader training. This page explains what Nooks and Orum actually offer for roleplay, when it is the right answer, and what to check in a standalone tool before you switch.

Everything here was read on the vendors' own websites in October 2026. We do not repeat prices third-party blogs claim for Nooks or Orum, because the vendors have not confirmed them.

## What Nooks AI Coaching does

Nooks describes itself as revenue infrastructure for reps and agents, and the AI Coaching module sits alongside the AI Dialer and AI Prospecting. The roleplay bots are built from real call recordings in the team's call library. The support documentation says you need at least five recorded calls of three minutes or more before a custom bot can be generated. After a real call, a rep can try again against a simulated version of that prospect. Bot personality can be set to friendly, rude or impossible, and the documentation suggests impossible is good for beat-the-bot incentives. Custom scorecards score roleplays and live calls on openers, objection handling, discovery questions and value proposition, with results logged to Salesforce or HubSpot.

That is a strong design if you dial on Nooks. The bots are grounded in your own calls because Nooks already has your calls. The weakness is the same fact in reverse. If you do not dial on Nooks, you have no call library there, and the roleplay comes with a dialler you did not ask for. Pricing is a custom quote, the company's own blog states a two-seat minimum, and third-party sources describe annual billing, which Nooks has not confirmed on its site.

## What Orum AI Coaching does

Orum calls itself a dialler first. Its AI Coaching and Enablement Suite, launched in 2025, adds roleplay agents, scorecards and a coaching portal. The roleplay agents are video avatars described as trained on the team's real conversations. Custom roleplays are created from a plain-language objective, and the difficulty is written into that prompt rather than chosen from fixed levels. Roleplay sessions are scored the same way live calls are, on openers, discovery, objections and tone, with a CSV export.

Orum's homepage FAQ states that pricing is per user and billed annually, with plans named Launch, Ascend and Ascend Limited, and AI Coaching available as an add-on to any plan. So the roleplay line depends on an annual dialler contract. Like Nooks, that is reasonable if the dialler is the purchase. It is a poor way to buy practice calls for a team that dials on something else.

## When the dialler route is right

Be honest about this before switching. If your team already dials on Nooks or Orum, the roleplay module is the lowest-friction option you have. The bots are built from calls you already recorded, scores land in the CRM you already use, and reps do not open a second tool. The question to ask your account manager is what the add-on costs per user, in writing, and whether it is tied to the dialler renewal. If the answer is a number you can afford and the bots sound like your buyers, stop reading.

The dialler route is wrong in three cases. You dial on something else and do not want to move. You want a manager to buy practice for a ramp class without a procurement cycle. Or your team records calls under EU, EEA or UK rules and cannot build a call library the bots need. Nooks' own guidance recommends turning call recording off for those teams, and its documentation notes that recording only the rep's audio breaks the scorecards.

## What to check in a standalone alternative

| Check | Why it matters | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Price on the website | A quote means a term and a minimum | A monthly seat price with the call allowance stated |
| Grounding without a call library | Nooks needs five recorded calls first | Prospects built from target accounts and uploaded transcripts |
| Fixed difficulty levels | Free-text difficulty makes scores incomparable | Named levels with behavioural briefs, the same for every rep |
| Prospect decides the outcome | Self-reported wins inflate | Booked, callback, rejected or hung up, judged from the prospect's words |
| Scoring consistency | Two reps need the same yardstick | A fixed rubric with the rationale quoting the rep |
| Data handling | Voice recordings are sensitive | Hosting region stated, no training on your data, deletion that reaches subprocessors |

100 Dials passes that list, which is why it is on this page. Prospects are built from the rep's real target accounts, and from the team's own transcripts if you upload CSV exports from Gong, Chorus, Fireflies or a dialler. There is no minimum call library. Three levels with fixed behavioural briefs run from a friendly prospect to a senior buyer who interrupts and tries to end the call twice. Every call is scored on six skills with the rationale quoting the rep's words, and sixteen objection types are tagged. Database, authentication and storage are hosted in the EU. The price is $79 per seat per month for 40 calls or $159 for 100, with a 10-call trial that needs a work email and no card. Our [manager page](/for-managers) shows the heatmap and objection table the scores feed. What it does not do is dial real prospects, log to a CRM or score your live calls. It is practice only.

Exec is another standalone option with a published price, $20 and $39 per seat on annual billing with seat minimums, built for sales, service and support training together rather than cold calls specifically. Hyperbound is standalone and voice-first with real-call scoring on its upper tier, sold by quote. ## Running practice next to a dialler you keep

The practical setup for most teams is a standalone simulator for practice and whatever dialler you already have for real calls. The handoff is manual but short. Export last quarter's call transcripts from the dialler as CSV and upload them so the practice prospect learns your market's objections. Have each rep run their practice calls before the morning dial block, on the skill their last scores flagged. Read the Monday digest for the objection the team fumbled most, then listen to two real calls where it came up. The simulator carries the repetitions and the dialler carries the pipeline, and neither vendor needs to know about the other.

<Practise>
Export ten real call transcripts from your dialler, upload them, and run five calls at the cold level. If the prospect raises the objection your team hears most, in roughly the words your market uses, the grounding works.
