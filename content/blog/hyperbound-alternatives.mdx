Hyperbound is a capable voice-first AI roleplay platform, and the reason people search for alternatives is rarely that it does not work. It is that Hyperbound is sold like enterprise software, per user by quote with volume and multi-year discounts, for a product many buyers want to try this week. The five alternatives below are sorted by how you buy them, because that is usually the real question. 100 Dials and Exec publish a price and let you start without a demo. Outdoo publishes a free tier and quotes the rest. Second Nature, Nooks and Orum sell by quote like Hyperbound, and each is the better pick for a specific kind of team. If your team makes cold calls and you want a price on the page, 100 Dials. If you train sales and service together and want the lowest seat price, Exec. If you need video avatars across an enterprise, Second Nature. If you are buying a dialler anyway, Nooks or Orum.

Everything here was read on each vendor's own website in October 2026. Where a vendor does not publish a price, we say so and do not repeat the figures third-party blogs claim.

## What Hyperbound does well, so you know what you are replacing

Hyperbound's pricing page lists a free tier with prebuilt roleplays, a Practice tier with custom buyer bots, reporting and custom scorecards, and a Perform tier that adds scoring of real calls. Personas are built from an ideal customer profile or a prospect's LinkedIn profile, informed by what Hyperbound describes as analysis of more than two million hours of B2B sales conversations. Scorecards can follow MEDDPICC, MEDDIC, BANT, Challenger, Sandler or SPIN. It supports 25 or more languages, integrates with Gong, Salesloft, Salesforce, HubSpot and SCORM-based LMS platforms, and publishes a trust centre with SOC 2, ISO 27001 and a GDPR addendum. It also writes a great many comparison pages about itself, including one ranking its own alternatives.

An alternative has to beat it on something specific. Here is where each does.

### 100 Dials: cold calls only, price on the page

100 Dials does one job. Reps dial a live AI prospect built from their own target accounts, at one of three fixed difficulty levels, and every call is scored on six cold-call skills with the rationale quoting the rep's words. Upload CSV exports of your real call transcripts and the prospects learn your market's objections in its phrasing. Managers get a heatmap of skills by rep, a table of the objections the team fumbles and a Monday digest. The price is $59 per seat per month for 40 calls or $159 for 100, monthly, 20% off annual, with a 10-call trial that needs a work email and no card. Database, authentication and storage are hosted in the EU.

Against Hyperbound it loses on languages, which it does not advertise, on scorecard flexibility, since the rubric is fixed on purpose, and on real-call scoring, which it does not do. It wins when the problem is cold calls, the team is small enough that a quote is friction, and you want to know what it costs before anyone calls you. Our [manager page](/for-managers) shows what the first two weeks look like.

### Exec: the lowest published seat price

Exec publishes its prices. A free tier gives five roleplay sessions per user per year. Starter is $20 per seat per month billed annually with a five-seat minimum and ten sessions per user per month. Professional is $39 with unlimited roleplays, custom rubrics and up to 50 custom scenarios. Exec is built for customer-facing teams generally, so a sales manager shares the platform with support and service training, and the scenarios include screen-shared demo practice. For a company that wants one training tool across departments at the lowest seat price, it is the clear alternative. For a cold-calling floor, the annual term and the session caps at the lower tier are the things to weigh.

### Outdoo: try before the quote

Outdoo publishes a free tier with 60 minutes of credits, no card and no sales call, and supports roleplay by voice, chat or video. Its paid tiers are listed as custom, so beyond the free hour you are back to a quote, and the company also sells revenue intelligence features such as deal health and forecasting that a practice buyer may not need. It is the alternative for a team that wants to hear several roleplay formats before committing to any vendor's process.

### Second Nature: enterprise video roleplay

Second Nature is the alternative when the people being trained are not SDRs. It runs video roleplays with an AI avatar, screen sharing and slides, builds scenarios from uploaded decks and scripts, and sells to insurance, telco, financial services and call-centre teams as well as sales. It is sold by demo only, with an enterprise package that includes setup training, and its FAQ says data is stored in a Netherlands data centre. If your need is a certified pitch delivered on video by two thousand agents, Second Nature fits it better than Hyperbound. If your need is cold calls, it is further from the mark. Our [comparison of Hyperbound, Second Nature and 100 Dials](/blog/hyperbound-vs-second-nature-vs-100-dials) goes through the two in detail.

### Nooks and Orum: roleplay inside the dialler

If your team dials on Nooks or Orum, the roleplay you already half-own is the obvious alternative. Nooks builds bots from the recorded calls in its library, needs at least five of them, and sets personality to friendly, rude or impossible. Orum's coaching suite adds video roleplay agents trained on your team's conversations as an add-on to its annual per-user plans. Neither publishes a price and neither sells roleplay without the dialler, so they are alternatives only for teams already on the platform. Our [Nooks AI roleplay alternative](/blog/nooks-ai-roleplay-alternative) page covers the standalone route for everyone else.

## How to choose in a week

Pick the two that fit your team from the table and run the same test on both. Have one rep make three calls at the hardest setting and read the transcripts: did the prospect try to end the call, and did it raise an objection your market actually uses. Have two managers score one call on paper before seeing the tool's score and check both land within a point. Count how many reps opened the tool on the second morning without being told. Then compare what it costs to find out you were wrong. With a published monthly price that is one month. With a multi-year quote it is the contract. That asymmetry, more than any feature, is why this page exists.

<Practise>
Run the three-call test on whichever tool you are trialling: three calls at the hardest level, transcripts read, one line you would change. If the prospect never tried to hang up, it is not hard enough to be worth paying for.
</Practise>
