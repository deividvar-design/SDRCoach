Most AI sales roleplay vendors do not publish a price. Their pricing pages name the tiers, describe licences priced per user with volume discounts by team size and extra savings for multi-year agreements, and ask you to book a demo for the number. That is not a dodge, it is how enterprise software is sold, and it tells you who the product is for. The way to buy well is to read the tier page for what it does say, understand what each piece of discount language does to your quote, and walk into the demo with nine questions on paper so you leave with a number you can compare. This page covers all three. We make a competing product with a published price, which you should weigh when reading it.

## How to read a pricing page with no prices

A quote-only pricing page still tells you a lot, if you read the nouns. Across the vendors we checked in October 2026, the tier structure follows the same shape, and the names vary more than the contents.

| Tier shape | What it usually includes | Who it is for | What to ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | A library of prebuilt roleplays, example scorecards, sometimes a session cap | Hearing the voice and the interruptions before any call with sales | Whether a free account can run a persona built from your own targets, or only the library |
| Practice | Custom buyer personas, roleplay customisation, reporting, custom scorecards | Teams that want reps practising against their own market | Seat minimum, term, roleplays per seat, whether persona building is included or a service |
| Performance | Everything in the practice tier, plus scoring of real recorded calls | Enablement teams that want live calls graded on the same rubric as practice | Which call platforms it pulls from, whether real-call scoring is metered, and the price gap from the practice tier |

The free tier is a demo you run yourself. It shows voice quality, latency and how the bot handles an interruption, which is worth knowing. It does not show the thing that decides whether reps improve, which is a prospect built from your own targets and scored on your own card. Both of those sit in the practice tier. So the free tier answers "does the voice sound real" and leaves "will my reps get better" for the paid conversation.

The practice tier is the one most SDR teams are actually shopping for. Custom personas mean buyers built from an ideal customer profile, a prospect's LinkedIn profile or your own call transcripts. Custom scorecards mean you can grade on your own card instead of the examples. Reporting is the manager view. If the job is cold-call practice, this is the line item.

The performance tier adds scoring of real calls. That is a different product with a different buyer. A head of enablement standardising a methodology across AEs and SDRs wants one rubric on practice calls and live calls alike, and this tier does that. A manager who wants eight SDRs ramping on the phone does not need it, and should not let it onto the quote by default. Our [page on which roleplay vendors publish a price](/blog/ai-sales-roleplay-software-pricing) lists what each vendor calls these tiers.

## What "per user, volume and multi-year" does to your quote

Three phrases carry the pricing model on almost every quote-only page. Each one shapes the number you will be given.

Per user means the quote scales with seats, and the first question back from the vendor will be how many. Give the number of reps who will dial it in month one, not the headcount on the org chart. A quote for forty seats when twelve reps will use it is a quote for twenty-eight idle licences.

Volume discounts by team size means the per-seat price falls as the seat count rises. The useful consequence is that the seat price you are quoted for eight reps is the top of the range, and the discount thresholds are worth asking for explicitly. If the next break is at twenty-five seats, you want to know that before you decide whether to include the AE team.

Additional savings for multi-year agreements is the phrase to read twice. A vendor that discounts a three-year term expects to sell three-year terms, and the first quote is likely to be framed against one. The annual price without the multi-year discount is the real comparison point, because it is the one that costs you a year, not three, if the reps do not dial it.

None of this makes a product expensive or cheap. It makes the price a negotiation, and negotiations reward the buyer who arrives with the questions written down.

## The nine questions that get you a number

Go into the demo with these on paper. A vendor that cannot answer one of them on the call will answer it in the follow-up email, which is where you want the number anyway.

- Which tier are we being quoted, and what is the price per seat on the tier above it, so we can see the gap.
- Is the term monthly, annual or multi-year, and what is the annual price with no multi-year discount applied.
- Is there a minimum seat count, and what are the volume discount thresholds.
- How many roleplays does a seat get per month, and is anything metered beyond the licence, such as minutes, sessions or real calls scored.
- Is building personas from our own targets, LinkedIn profiles or call transcripts included in the tier, or is it a services engagement with its own line.
- Is onboarding, scorecard setup or integration with our call recorder, dialler and CRM charged separately.
- What is the renewal uplift, and is the discount we are being offered locked at renewal or does the price reset.
- Where is customer data hosted, and is it used to train models. A trust centre that lists certifications but no hosting region is common, and a European buyer should ask.
- Can we have all of the above in writing before the call ends.

The last question is the one that matters. A verbal number from a demo is not a price. A number in an email with the term, the seat count and the tier next to it is.

## Which tier a cold-calling SDR team needs

If your reps make cold calls and your goal is a faster ramp, the answer is the practice tier, for the number of reps who will dial it in the first month, on an annual term rather than multi-year, with persona building confirmed as included. Use the free tier for a week first, so the demo is spent on your personas and not on hearing the voice.

Real-call scoring earns its place when you already record every live call, you have a scorecard the whole revenue team agrees on, and you want the practice score and the live score to be the same number. That is a real need for a large revenue organisation. It is not a need for a floor of six to thirty SDRs who are still trying to get the opener right, and it is the line that most inflates a quote for that floor.

Before the tier question, check the product itself against the four things that decide whether reps improve. Our [buyer's guide to AI cold call practice software](/blog/ai-cold-call-practice-software) sets them out, and a tool that fails them is not worth a quote at any tier.

## How to compare the quote with a published price

The honest comparison is not seat price against seat price. It is what it costs to find out whether the tool works.

With a quote on an annual term, the cost of being wrong is the contract. With a published monthly price, it is one month. 100 Dials, which is the product we make, publishes $79 per seat per month for 40 calls or $159 for 100, with 20% off annual, a 10-call trial that needs a work email and no card, and a 30-day money-back guarantee on the first payment. Calls are pooled across seats, so a rep who dials more than the others does not need a second licence. Prospects are built from the rep's own target accounts and from the team's own call transcripts, which is the grounding that quote-only vendors sell inside their practice tier, and our [enablement page](/for-enablement) describes how the upload works. The rubric is fixed to six cold-call skills on purpose, which is also the reason to pick a quote-only platform instead if the scorecard has to follow a named methodology, the team sells in many languages, or live calls need grading.

Put the two side by side on the same sheet. For the quote column, use the annual practice-tier price per seat for your real seat count, plus any persona-building or onboarding line, divided by twelve. For the published column, use the monthly seat price times the same seats. Then write the term next to each. If the first column is a year and the second is a month, that difference is the thing you are buying with a quote, and it is worth seeing in ink before you sign.
