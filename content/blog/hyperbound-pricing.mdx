Hyperbound does not publish a price. Its pricing page, as of October 2026, lists three tiers named Free, Practice and Perform, says licences are priced per user with Practice and Perform sold separately, offers volume discounts by team size and additional savings for multi-year agreements, and ends with a button to contact the team for custom pricing. That is everything the company says about cost. Every dollar figure you will find for Hyperbound on a comparison blog is a guess or a secondhand quote, and none is confirmed by the vendor. So the useful question is not "how much is Hyperbound" but "what am I being quoted for, and what do I need in writing before I sign." This page answers that.

We read the Hyperbound website directly. We do not repeat prices from third parties, because for a quote-only vendor those numbers are usually one buyer's deal, one year old, with the seat count and term stripped off.

## The three tiers and what each one buys

| Tier | What it adds | Who it is for | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | Prebuilt roleplays, example scorecards | A rep or manager who wants to hear the product before a demo | Free |
| Practice | Custom buyer bots built from your ICP or a LinkedIn profile, custom scorecards, reporting | A team practising discovery, cold and demo calls against its own market | Per user, by quote |
| Perform | Everything in Practice plus scoring of real calls, with call recorder and CRM integrations | A revenue organisation that wants practice and live calls graded on one scorecard | Per user, by quote, a separate licence |

The free tier is a listening test. You cannot build your own prospect on it, so what you learn is whether the voice sounds like a person, whether the latency bothers your reps and how a scorecard reads. That is worth an hour. It is not a trial of the thing you would pay for.

Practice is the licence most SDR teams are actually buying. It is where the buyer bot stops being generic and starts being built from your ideal customer profile, where the scorecard follows MEDDPICC, BANT, SPIN or your own rubric, and where a manager can see results across the team. If your question is cold-call practice, Practice is the quote to ask for and Perform is an upsell you can decline.

Perform is a different product that happens to share the scorecard. It scores recorded real calls from Gong, Salesloft or your dialler against the same rubric as the roleplays. That is useful when you want a rep's practice score and their live score on one chart. It is also the tier where the integrations live, so if you need the Salesforce or HubSpot connection for the practice side alone, ask whether that comes with Practice or only with Perform.

## What the pricing page's words tell you before the demo

Four phrases on that page decide the shape of your quote, and it pays to know what each one means before a salesperson explains it to you.

"Priced per user" means the number scales with headcount, not usage. Ask whether a user is a named seat that can be reassigned when a rep leaves, and how quickly. SDR teams turn over. A seat you cannot move is a seat you pay for twice during ramp season.

"Separate licences for Practice and Perform" means you can mix. The default quote will often put every seat on the higher tier. Ask for Practice on the floor and Perform on the people whose live calls you actually review, or for Practice alone.

"Volume discounts based on team size" means the per-seat price you are first told is the small-team price. Ask where the breaks are. If the next break is at 25 seats and you have 18, you want to know that before you decide how many managers to include.

"Additional savings for multi-year agreements" is the phrase to read twice. A vendor that discounts a three-year term expects to sell three-year terms. The first number you hear may already assume one. Ask for the one-year price and the monthly equivalent of each, in writing, so you are comparing like with like against any tool that publishes a monthly seat price.

## Why no number online is worth repeating

Search for Hyperbound pricing and you will find figures. They come from three places. Some are a buyer's own deal, posted with the seat count and term left out, which makes the per-seat figure meaningless. Some are review-site estimates that one blog copied from another until it looked like consensus. One analysis we read in October 2026 traced the figure currently circulating to somebody's published guess. None of these came from Hyperbound.

That matters because a quote-only vendor prices each deal. The same product can be quoted at different per-seat rates to two companies in the same month, depending on seats, term, tiers mixed and which quarter the rep is closing. A number from someone else's quote tells you what they paid, not what you will. Treat every figure that is not on an invoice addressed to you as noise, including any you hear from us.

## What to ask for in the demo, in writing

Go in with the questions written down and leave with the answers in an email, not a slide. The ones that change the total are these.

Which tier is this price for, and is it one price or two. If Perform is included, ask for the same quote without it.

Is the term one year or longer, and what is the price at renewal. A first-year discount that reverts is a price rise you are agreeing to today.

How many seats does the quoted rate require, and what happens if you drop below. Minimum seat counts hide in volume pricing.

What is metered. Hyperbound sells per user, but ask whether roleplay minutes, bot builds or real-call scoring volume have any cap or overage on the tier you are buying.

Is building bots from your own call data part of the licence or a services engagement. Grounding the prospect in your own market is the feature that decides whether reps improve, as we set out on our [enablement page](/for-enablement), and on some platforms it sits in onboarding services rather than the seat. Ask which it is here.

Where is the data hosted. Hyperbound publishes SOC 2, ISO 27001 and a GDPR addendum, and its site does not state a hosting region. If your security team needs one, ask for it in writing before the price discussion, because a regional commitment can change the quote.

Can the number be in an email before the call ends. A vendor that will not put a price in writing in the first conversation is telling you how the renewal conversation will go.

## How to budget when there is no price

You can still put a line in a budget without a quote, by working from the shape of the deal rather than the figure. Decide the seat count you would actually roll out, not the pilot. Decide the longest term you would sign without seeing results, which for most sales managers we talk to is one year. Then write down what it would cost to be wrong: with an annual, per-user, multi-year-discounted contract, the cost of a tool the reps stop opening in week three is the rest of the contract.

That last number is the honest comparison between a quote-only tool and a tool with a published monthly seat price. On the published side you can read the figure, start the trial and know within a month. On the quoted side you need a demo, a proposal and a signature before the first practice call, and the exit is the term. Which is right depends on the size of the programme, and for a large revenue organisation standardising hundreds of seats the quote process is normal procurement. For a manager with eight SDRs it is most of the work. ## When Hyperbound is worth going through the quote

Price opacity is a reason to be careful, not a reason to walk away. Hyperbound is the right tool to be quoted for when your programme spans SDRs, AEs and customer success with different methodology scorecards, when reps sell in many languages, when you want real calls scored on the same rubric as practice, or when SCORM and LMS integration is a requirement. In those cases the Perform tier is doing a job that cheaper, narrower tools do not do, and a per-user annual contract is the normal way to buy it.

If your problem is narrower, specifically cold calls for an SDR team, the quote process is buying you capability you will not use. 100 Dials, for example, publishes $79 per seat per month for 40 calls and trials on ten free calls with no sales call, and does only cold calls. Our [page on Hyperbound alternatives](/blog/hyperbound-alternatives) sorts the options by how you buy them and is honest about where Hyperbound still wins. Read it alongside the quote, not instead of it.
