Every conversation-intelligence tool reports talk ratio, and every sales manager has a number they believe in. Usually it is borrowed from discovery calls, where the prospect should do most of the talking. Cold calls are not discovery calls, and applying the same target produces reps who ask a stranger "what keeps you up at night?" fifteen seconds into an interruption.

## Cold calls are a pitch that becomes a conversation

Gong's analysis of successful cold calls found a distinct shape: they begin with the rep talking, because the rep has to establish who they are and why they are calling, and they turn into a dialogue as the prospect engages. Successful cold calls ran longer than unsuccessful ones, around five to six minutes rather than under four, and the difference was made of back-and-forth, not of longer pitches.

So the right talk ratio for a cold call is not 30 percent. In the first minute it is close to 80. Over the whole call it lands somewhere between 40 and 60. What separates good calls from bad ones inside that range is not the ratio. It is the shape.

## The number that matters: longest monologue

A rep can hit 50 percent talk time two ways: twelve short turns, or one two-minute pitch and a lot of listening to the prospect say no. The ratio is identical. The calls are not.

Prospects on a cold call tune out after about thirty seconds of uninterrupted rep speech. After forty-five seconds they are composing their exit line. A reliable coaching rule: no single rep turn over thirty seconds until the prospect has asked you a question. If you need more than thirty seconds to explain what you do, the explanation is the problem.

## Three stats worth tracking instead of the ratio alone

1. **Longest rep monologue.** Under thirty seconds is the target. This is the single best predictor of whether the prospect stayed engaged.
2. **Questions asked.** Two to four in a five-minute cold call. Fewer means you pitched. More means you ran discovery on someone who did not agree to it.
3. **Time to first objection.** Early is fine, it means the prospect is engaged enough to push back. What matters is what happened next: did the rep acknowledge it before answering?

## What to do with the number

Do not coach the ratio. Coach the turn. Pull up the longest monologue on the transcript, read it aloud with the rep, and cut it in half together. Then have them run the same call again. The ratio takes care of itself once the turns are short.
