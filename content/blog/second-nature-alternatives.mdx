Second Nature is an enterprise AI roleplay platform built around video: a rep practises on a call with an avatar, shares a screen, runs through slides, and is evaluated on messaging, tone and clarity. It is sold by demo, with pricing tailored to the use case and an enterprise package that includes training sessions. Teams look for alternatives for one of three reasons. They want a price without a demo. They want voice cold calls rather than video pitches. Or they are a sales team of ten, not a training department of two thousand. The alternatives below map to those reasons. 100 Dials is the cold-call alternative with a published monthly price and a trial. Exec is the lowest published seat price for broad training. Hyperbound is the enterprise voice alternative, also sold by quote. Outdoo offers a free hour of voice, chat and video before its quote. Nooks and Orum bundle roleplay into a dialler.

Everything here was read on each vendor's own website in October 2026. Where a vendor publishes no price, we say so and do not repeat figures that third-party blogs claim.

## What Second Nature is good at

It is worth being clear about what you would be giving up. Second Nature runs 3D avatars with configurable mood and temperament. It supports 30 or more languages. It integrates with Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoom, Active Directory and SCORM or LTI learning systems. It lists SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001 and ISO 27701, and its FAQ states data is stored in a Google Cloud data centre in the Netherlands and is never used for model training. Its homepage claims a 34% reduction in ramp time and 46% more deals closed, which are vendor claims and should be read as such. For an insurer rolling out a new product pitch, a telco retention script or a bank's compliance conversation on video, it is built for the job and the alternatives below are not.

## The alternatives, by reason for leaving

| Reason you are looking | Alternative | What it publishes |

### 100 Dials: cold calls, a monthly price, EU hosting

100 Dials is built for SDR teams making cold calls and nothing else. Reps dial a live AI prospect built from their own target accounts at one of three fixed difficulty levels. Upload CSV exports of your real call transcripts and the prospect learns the objections your market raises, in its phrasing. Every call is scored on six cold-call skills, weighted toward what books meetings, with the rationale quoting the rep's own words, and managers get a skills heatmap, an objection table and a Monday digest. The price is $59 per seat per month for 40 calls or $159 for 100, monthly with 20% off annual, and the trial is 10 calls over 14 days with a work email and no card. Database, authentication and file storage are in the EU, and enterprise customers can pin voice and language processing to EU regions.

What it does not do matters here. There is no video, no screen share, no slides, no language list and no certification workflow. If a Second Nature scenario in your programme is a demo rather than a conversation, 100 Dials cannot replace it. If the scenario is a cold call, it replaces it with a prospect who interrupts. ### Exec: broad training at the lowest published price

Exec publishes a free tier of five sessions per user per year, a Starter tier at $20 per seat per month billed annually with a five-seat minimum and ten sessions per user per month, and a Professional tier at $39 with unlimited roleplays and custom rubrics. It covers sales, service and support training and includes screen-shared demo practice, which makes it the closest published-price alternative to Second Nature's breadth. The trade-offs are the annual term, the seat minimums and the capped sessions at the lower tier.

### Hyperbound: enterprise voice, also by quote

Hyperbound is the alternative for a team that wants Second Nature's enterprise fit with a voice-first product and sales-specific scorecards. Personas are built from ideal customer profiles and LinkedIn data, scorecards follow MEDDPICC, MEDDIC, BANT and other frameworks, it supports 25 or more languages, and the upper tier scores real calls. It is sold per user by quote with volume and multi-year discounts, so it solves the format question and not the pricing one. Our [comparison of Hyperbound, Second Nature and 100 Dials](/blog/hyperbound-vs-second-nature-vs-100-dials) covers the two side by side, and our [Hyperbound alternatives](/blog/hyperbound-alternatives) page covers the rest of the field.

### Outdoo: a free hour before any quote

Outdoo publishes a free tier with 60 minutes of credits, no card, no sales call, across voice, chat and video roleplay. Its paid tiers are listed as custom. It is a sensible first stop for a team that wants to hear what each format sounds like before sitting through anyone's demo, Second Nature's included.

### Nooks and Orum: if the dialler is the purchase

Nooks builds roleplay bots from the recorded calls in its dialler library and sets their personality to friendly, rude or impossible. Orum adds video roleplay agents as an add-on to its annual per-user plans. Neither publishes a price or sells roleplay without the platform. They are alternatives for teams that already dial on them and want practice without a second tool.

## The test that settles it

Shortlist two from the table and run the same test on both before any pricing conversation. One rep, three calls at the hardest setting, transcripts read: did the prospect push back the way your buyers do. Two managers score one call on paper before seeing the tool's score: did both land within a point. Second morning: how many reps opened it unprompted. Then ask what it costs to be wrong. A published monthly price makes that one month. A demo-led enterprise package makes it the contract. For a training department buying for the whole company, that may be fine. For a sales manager with a ramp class starting Monday, it is the reason to look elsewhere.

Have your newest rep run three calls at the cold level today and bring you the transcript where the prospect tried to end the call. That is the conversation your programme should be training, whichever tool you pick.
