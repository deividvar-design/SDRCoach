An average SDR connects on about 5% of dials and turns about 5% of those connects into a meeting. A top-quartile rep connects on 13% and converts 17%, which is roughly four times the output from the same list. The median SDR hits quota 60% of the time, ramps in three months and stays under two years. Those are the cold calling statistics that matter in 2026, and every figure on this page is traced to the organisation that measured it, with the year and the size of the dataset where the source gives one. Most of the data comes from platform vendors, Gong, Cognism, Orum, Chorus and ZoomInfo, measuring calls made through their software. That is not peer-reviewed research and we say so. It is still the largest body of evidence on cold calls that exists. At the end is a list of eight famous cold calling statistics that no one can source, so you can stop citing them.

We will add figures from our own practice calls once the sample is large enough to mean something. For now this page is other people's data, labelled.

## Openers and the first thirty seconds

| Statistic | What it tells you | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "How have you been?" booked at 10.01% against a 1.5% baseline | A familiar-sounding opener outperformed every other line in the set | [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines), 90,380 calls |
| Stating "the reason for my call" raised success 2.1 times | The single most reliable line on a cold call | Gong Labs, 90,380 calls |
| "Did I catch you at a bad time?" booked at 0.9%, 40% below baseline | The polite exit you hand the prospect | [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/cold-calling), 90,380 calls |
| In the later 300M-call study the same line was the lowest validated opener at 2.15% | Different baseline, same ranking | [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls), 2024 |
| Permission-based openers booked at 11.18%, "heard your name tossed around" at 11.24%, "how's your day going" at 7.6% | Asking for thirty seconds works. Small talk works less well | Gong Labs, 300M+ calls, 2024 |
| The median successful cold call lasts 4.8 minutes and handles at least two objections | A booked meeting is usually the third yes, not the first | [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all), 2024 |

The two Gong studies use different baselines, so do not compare the 0.9% and 2.15% figures for the same opener directly. Our post on [the best and worst cold call openers](/blog/best-cold-call-openers) goes through what to say instead.

## Timing

| Statistic | What it tells you | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Best success rates between 11am and noon and between 4pm and 5pm, prospect's local time | Late morning and the end of the day | Gong Labs, 300M+ calls, 2024 |
| Highest connect rates on Tuesday and Wednesday, 9am to noon | Trailing three months of dialler data | [Gong Engage benchmarks](https://help.gong.io/docs/engage-analytics-benchmarks-and-best-practices), 2025 |
| Tuesday was the best day for meetings booked. 10 to 11am the best hour, 2 to 3pm second | | Cognism, 2025 |
| Thursday was the best day to get through, Tuesday second. Clear drop before 9am and after 4pm | The best day moved year on year. The dead zones did not | Cognism, 2026 |

| Mondays had a slightly higher connect rate than any other weekday | The counterpoint, from a different platform | Orum, 2024 |
| 37% of companies responded to a web lead within an hour, 24% took more than a day, 23% never responded | 2,241 US companies audited | [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, 2011 |

The widely quoted claim that 4pm to 6pm is 114% better than mid-morning comes from a 2007 study and is contradicted by every dataset above. Late morning wins in the current data.

## Eight cold calling statistics that have no source

These appear in almost every statistics roundup. We looked for the original study behind each one and could not find it.

| The claim | What we found |
|---|---|
| "92% of all customer interactions happen over the phone" | No study. The earliest instance is a 2015 listicle that cites nothing |
| "It takes 8 cold call attempts to reach a prospect" | Attributed to a 2007 study that measured 3.68. The "8" has no publication behind it. Cognism measured 2.9 in 2025 and 1.55 in 2026 |
| "80% of sales require 5 follow-up calls" and "44% of reps give up after one follow-up" | Two marketing blogs from around 2016, no dataset, and the numbers change between sites |
| "80% of calls go to voicemail and 90% of first voicemails are never returned" | One vendor cited by one article. Gong's voicemail data above is the traceable version |
| "Reps forget 70% of training within a week and 87% within a month" | Attributed to Gartner and to Xerox. Neither published it. Use the forgetting-curve replication above |
| "48% of salespeople are afraid to cold call" | Attributed to Gartner. The nearest real survey is ValueSelling's 39% |
| "Reps coached three or more hours a month hit 107% of quota" | Not on the Sales Management Association's site or in its reports |
| "4pm to 6pm is 114% better than mid-morning for cold calls" | A 2007 study. Gong, Cognism and ZoomInfo all now show late morning as the best window |

If you cite this page, cite the source in the row, not us. We will check every link again when the 2027 edition is published and replace any figure its owner has updated.
