"Just send me an email" is not an objection. It is a polite way of ending the call, and the prospect expects you to accept it. Most reps do: they say "sure, what's the best address?", send an email nobody opens, and log the call as a follow-up. Nothing was lost except the meeting.

The response that works treats the line as what it is, a soft no, and answers the no rather than the request.

## Why folding is so tempting

The prospect has offered you a graceful exit. Taking it feels like progress: you got an email address, you have a reason to follow up, the call ended without anyone being rude. Every one of those is a consolation prize. Across Gong's cold-call data, the calls that booked meetings were the ones that turned into a back-and-forth. An email is the opposite of a back-and-forth.

## The four steps

**1. Agree, briefly.**
Fighting the request makes you sound like a telemarketer. "Happy to." One beat, no more.

**2. Ask what to put in it.**
This is the whole technique. "So I don't send you something useless, what's the one thing you'd actually want to see in it?" Now the prospect has to think about their problem, out loud, with you on the line. Most people answer. The ones who say "anything is fine" have told you they are not a prospect, which is also useful.

**3. Answer the thing they said, in one sentence.**
They said "pricing"? "It's per vehicle per month, typically forty to eighty, and I can tell you where you'd land in about two questions." They said "how it works"? Give the one-line mechanism, then stop.

**4. Ask for the small next step, with a time.**
"Rather than go back and forth over email, I've got fifteen minutes Thursday at ten. If it isn't relevant you'll know in five." A specific slot converts far better than "sometime next week", because a specific slot is a yes-or-no question.

## The words, together

> Prospect: Just send me an email.
>
> Rep: Happy to. So it's not something you delete, what's the one thing you'd want it to answer?
>
> Prospect: Honestly, whether it works with what we already have.
>
> Rep: Fair. It sits next to your existing tooling rather than replacing it; most teams keep both. Quickest way to see if that's true for you is fifteen minutes on Thursday at ten. Does that work, or is Friday better?

## When to actually send the email

If they answer step two with something real and still decline the meeting, send the email, and send exactly what they asked for, in three sentences, with the meeting link at the bottom. Then call again in a week. You are no longer a cold call; you are the person who did what they said.

<Practise>
Ask the Level 2 inbound persona a discovery question and it will hit you with "send me something" within a minute. Run it five times and use only the four steps. Your objection-handling score is graded on whether you acknowledged, isolated and redirected, not on whether the prospect said yes.
</Practise>
